2D Wind Vertex Shader

Vertex Displacement GPU Optimization Procedural Noise

The Goal

To breathe life into 2D environments (foliage, cloth) without relying on expensive CPU-based physical simulations or heavy skeletal animations. The solution needed to be fully processed on the GPU.

How It Works

Instead of animating the sprite, I built a Shader Graph that manipulates the object's vertices in real-time. By driving a Gradient Noise node with a Time and Speed parameter, I created an organic waving pattern.

The core challenge was keeping the bottom of the plants attached to the ground. To solve this, I split the UV coordinates and used the vertical gradient as a mathematical mask. By multiplying the noise output by this UV mask, the displacement force drops to zero at the root, while the top of the sprite receives the full sway effect. Finally, this data is added directly to the X-axis of the object's Vertex Position.

Sobel Outline & Edge Detection

HLSL Function Normal/Depth Buffer Full-Screen Post-Process

The Goal

Fast-paced melee combat requires excellent visual readability. The goal was to create a striking, comic-book aesthetic that draws thick, stylized outlines around characters and environmental hazards to make silhouettes pop.

How It Works

I utilized a Full-Screen Render Feature to apply the effect globally across the camera view. The system relies on a dual-shader approach.

One part of the logic handles depth, while the other samples the URP Normal Buffer based on screen position. I integrated a custom HLSL Sobel function to trace the hard geometric edges and creases detected in the buffers. Because it is calculated as a full-screen post-process pass, the performance cost remains stable regardless of the physical polycount in the scene.

Thermal Vision (X-Ray)

Custom Render Pass Layer Masking Depth Culling

The Goal

Developed specifically for the gunner role in an asymmetric multiplayer game. The gunner needed a high-contrast thermal view capable of spotting enemy heat signatures through physical walls and geometry.

How It Works

I built a URP Full Screen Render Feature that relies heavily on strict Layer and Tag separation. By separating the enemy data from the environmental geometry layers, the shader can accurately isolate targets.

To achieve the "X-Ray" effect, the shader modifies the standard depth logic. It intentionally bypasses depth culling for the specific enemy layers, allowing their silhouettes to render flawlessly on top of the environment, regardless of the physical walls standing between them and the camera.

Dynamic Impact Frames

C# Integration Material Overrides

The Goal

To drastically improve the sense of weight and "punchiness" during heavy combat. The visual feedback needed to sync perfectly with the game's mechanical hit-stop (time freeze) logic.

How It Works

Unlike continuous ambient shaders, this is a highly targeted event. It is triggered dynamically via C# scripts tied directly to the combat animation state machine.

Upon a successful heavy hit, the logic temporarily overrides the character materials, flashing the screen with a high-contrast, stylized color palette. Once the hit-stop frames conclude, the system seamlessly returns the materials to their standard rendering state.
