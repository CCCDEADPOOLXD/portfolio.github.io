Sightless

A sensory horror game developed in 48 hours for the BYOG game jam. Players must navigate a pitch-black procedural maze using real-world microphone input to cast sonar waves, avoiding an AI that hunts based on shifting rules.

The Mechanics

Sensory Deprivation & AI Flipping

The core gameplay loop is built entirely around risk versus reward through sound generation.

  • Mic-Driven Echolocation: Players are completely blind. They must physically speak into their microphone (or use RMB) to cast sonar waves and reveal the environment.
  • NavMesh AI Rules: Initially, the enemy can only move inside the illuminated echolocation waves, forcing the player to be careful with how much they reveal.
  • The Flip: Halfway through the game, upon collecting the objective, the rules invert. The enemy now hunts the player *within* the echolocation rings and freezes in the dark, making every sound a massive risk.

Tech Art: Sonar Shader

Illuminating the Dark

To achieve the blindness effect, I engineered a custom HLSL echolocation shader that remains entirely black until triggered by an external audio event.

  • World-Space Masking: The shader calculates the world-space distance from the sound's origin. It illuminates only the physical geometry that falls inside the active acoustic ring, keeping the rest of the maze hidden.
  • C# Integration: A custom manager actively monitors the player's microphone amplitude and passes that volume data directly to the shader to spawn the expanding sphere.

Procedural Gen & Editor Tools

Automating the Pipeline

With only 48 hours to build the game, manually building a massive backrooms maze and assigning shaders was not an option.

  • Procedural Backrooms: I developed a procedural level generator that pieces together given assets to create a vast, unpredictable maze on initialization.
  • Material Batch Converter: To apply the echolocation shader to the thousands of generated assets, I engineered a C# Editor tool that recursively scans standard materials, swaps them to the Sonar shader, and automatically reassigns the original Albedo/Normal maps.
